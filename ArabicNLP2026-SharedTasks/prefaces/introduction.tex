Welcome to the proceedings of the Shared Tasks at the \textbf{Third Arabic Natural Language Processing Conference (ArabicNLP 2025)}, co-located with \textbf{EMNLP 2025} in Suzhou, China, November 8–9, 2025.


This volume represents a major achievement in collaborative Arabic NLP research, bringing together a total of \textbf{138 papers from 11 shared tasks} — including \textbf{11 overview papers} and \textbf{127 system description papers} from participating teams worldwide. This substantial collection reflects the growing vitality and maturity of the Arabic NLP research community and showcases the field’s expansion into increasingly diverse and sophisticated challenges.

This collection highlights the community’s growing interest toward evaluating, aligning, and extending these models for Arabic — across text, speech, and multimodal domains, as well as culturally grounded and ethically sensitive applications.
This year marks a record milestone for ArabicNLP, featuring the largest number of shared tasks to date — \textbf{11 in total}. The process began with \textbf{26 submitted shared task proposals}, from which \textbf{11 shared tasks were accepted}, including \textbf{6 formed through successful mergers}. This outcome underscores the community’s strong spirit of collaboration and its collective effort to design impactful, high-quality shared tasks.


\subsubsection*{Organization and Participation of the Shared Tasks}

The 11 shared tasks at ArabicNLP 2025 are organized into three thematic tracks, each addressing critical needs and emerging priorities in Arabic language technology. \textbf{Together, they represent the community’s most comprehensive shared task effort to date, covering speech, multimodal processing, text quality, and cultural and ethical evaluation in the era of large language models (LLMs).}

\paragraph{\textbf{Track 1: Speech and Multimodal Processing}}

This track features four shared tasks that advance Arabic language processing beyond traditional text:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ImageEval (Arabic Image Captioning)} — 8 papers
    \item \textbf{Iqra’Eval (Qur’anic Pronunciation Assessment)} — 5 papers
    \item \textbf{NADI 2025 (Multidialectal Arabic Speech Processing)} — 7 papers
    \item \textbf{MAHED 2025 (Multimodal Detection of Hope and Hate Emotions in Arabic Content)} — 23 papers

 These tasks address the pressing need for technologies that can process Arabic across different modalities and dialectal variations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{\textbf{Track 2: Text Quality and Generation Assessment}}

This track comprises four shared tasks focused on evaluating and enhancing Arabic text quality:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{AraGenEval (Arabic Authorship Style Transfer and AI-Generated Text Detection)} — 16 papers
    \item \textbf{TAQEEM 2025 (Arabic Quality Evaluation of Essays in Multi-dimensions)} — 4 papers
    \item \textbf{BAREC 2025 (Arabic Readability Assessment)} — 17 papers
    \item \textbf{AraHealthQA 2025 (Comprehensive Arabic Health Question Answering)} — 15 papers

 These tasks tackle essential challenges in automated assessment, generation evaluation, and domain-specific question answering.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{\textbf{Track 3: Cultural and Ethical Evaluation of LLMs for Arabic}}

This track introduces three groundbreaking shared tasks designed to evaluate large language models’ understanding of Arabic culture and Islamic knowledge:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{IslamicEval (Capturing LLMs’ Hallucination in Islamic Content)} — 7 papers
    \item \textbf{PalmX 2025 (Benchmarking LLMs on Arabic and Islamic Culture)} — 9 papers
    \item \textbf{QIAS 2025 (Islamic Inheritance Reasoning and Knowledge Assessment)} — 16 papers

 These tasks represent a critical step toward ensuring that AI systems appropriately and accurately represent the cultural and religious contexts of Arabic-speaking communities.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Community Impact and Participation}}

The remarkable response to this year’s shared tasks — with \textbf{127 system submissions across the 11 tasks} — demonstrates the Arabic NLP community’s continued growth and dynamism. Participating teams represent a diverse range of institutions across multiple continents, including universities, research centers, and industry partners, reflecting both the global interest in Arabic NLP and the real-world relevance of these challenges.

The breadth of methodological approaches presented in these proceedings is particularly noteworthy, spanning traditional machine learning techniques, state-of-the-art transformer models, retrieval-augmented generation systems, and multimodal architectures. This methodological diversity not only reflects the field’s technical maturity but also highlights researchers’ creativity in addressing the unique challenges posed by Arabic language processing.
